## About Your Closed Test

### How easy was it to recruit testers?

Draft: Moderately easy. I recruited testers from personal contacts and people interested in Japanese media/language learning. I targeted Android users who could realistically test Japanese video captioning, internal audio capture, microphone fallback, and floating overlays.

### Engagement received

Draft: Testers installed the app, completed setup, ran internal audio preflight on Japanese videos, tested floating captions, and used mic fallback when internal audio was blocked. I asked testers to report their device, Android version, capture result, overlay behavior, and caption readability.

### Feedback summary

Draft: Feedback focused on internal audio compatibility by app/source, overlay behavior in portrait and landscape, setup clarity, caption readability, and fallback behavior. The app includes Capture Troubleshooting and Share Support Diagnostics to reduce confusion when Android playback capture is blocked by the source app.

## About The App

### Intended audience

Draft: Android users who watch Japanese videos, streams, live chat, or educational media and want English captions. This includes Japanese learners and viewers of Japanese-language online content.

### Value to users

Draft: The app provides a floating English transcript over media playing in other apps. It uses free local Japanese-to-English captioning, internal audio capture when Android and the source app allow it, and microphone fallback when internal audio is blocked. It helps users understand Japanese media without leaving the video.

### Expected installs in first year

Draft: Choose the smallest realistic Play Console range unless you already have a marketing channel.
